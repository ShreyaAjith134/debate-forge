Nuclear power produces virtually zero carbon emissions during operation. A single nuclear plant generates as much electricity as hundreds of wind turbines while occupying a fraction of the land.

France generates over 70% of its electricity from nuclear power and has some of the lowest electricity costs and carbon emissions in Europe, demonstrating nuclear viability at scale.

Modern reactor designs including small modular reactors are significantly safer than older designs. The technology has advanced dramatically since Chernobyl and Fukushima.

Nuclear power provides reliable baseload electricity 24/7 regardless of weather conditions. Unlike solar and wind, it doesn't require expensive storage solutions to maintain grid stability.

Uranium mining and enrichment carry significant environmental costs. The full lifecycle carbon footprint of nuclear energy, including construction and fuel processing, is higher than often claimed.

Nuclear waste remains radioactive for thousands of years. No country has yet built a permanent geological repository. We are creating a burden for hundreds of future generations with no guaranteed solution.

Construction costs and timelines for nuclear plants have consistently overrun projections. In the US and UK, recent nuclear projects have cost two to three times their original budgets.

The risk of accidents, while low, carries catastrophic consequences. Fukushima's economic and social damage, including mass displacement, exceeded any savings from decades of cheap power.
